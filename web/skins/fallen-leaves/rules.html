There are ten kinds of leaf (oak, chestnut, ginkgo, elm, willow, maple, aspen, hawthorn, ivy and ash), each in four colours: green, yellow, orange and red. Hold one kind of leaf in all four colours at the end of your turn and you win.

The table has five rows. The far row is your opponent's leaves, the near row is yours, and the three between are the middle. The piles are at the side, one for each colour; the rims beside each pile are the leaves still beneath it.

Pick. Drag a face-up leaf from a pile to your row. You may keep only one leaf of each colour, so if you now hold two, drag either one to a glowing place in the middle. A leaf goes at the left or right end of a row, and a column must be full before the next one starts.

Match. If a leaf on the row of the middle nearest you is the same kind as one you hold, tap it to match along a diagonal rising from it. Its three leaves come up into your hand. Put three back, in any order, from everything you now hold; the match is made when the third goes down. You must end with one of each colour. Put them back as they were to change your mind.

Your opponent sits opposite, so their near row is the far row of the middle. You may also pass.
